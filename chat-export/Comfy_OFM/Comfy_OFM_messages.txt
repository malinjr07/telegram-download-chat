i connected three of the sam3 nodes together in the same module

2026-04-26 04:43:13 Comfy OFM VAULT 💰 -> Comfy OFM:
been experimenting with a Z-Image workflow lately and wanted to break down three nodes/patterns that are doing a lot of the heavy lifting under the hood.

GenerateNoise (from kjnodes) is essentially replacing the standard empty latent. instead of just handing the sampler a blank slate, it generates structured noise that's already model-aware — it takes the model and your resolution directly, so the noise is shaped correctly for the architecture from the start. in this setup i'm piping width/height straight from ZImageTurboResolutions so the latent is always locked to the right aspect ratio (1152x2048 in this case).

LGNoiseInjectionLatent (from comfyui_lg_samplingutils) is the interesting one. it takes the generated noise as a reference latent and injects it into the model itself — not the latent going into the sampler, the model. what this does is bias the sampling process toward the noise structure you defined earlier, giving you more consistency across seeds rather than fully random diffusion paths. settings i landed on: 0.4 strength, 0 shift, 0.2 end — that last value is key, keeps the injection from polluting the later denoising steps.

how did i use these together?

GenerateNoise → LGNoiseInjectionLatent (as reference)

same GenerateNoise latent also feeds directly into KSampler 1 as the starting latent

LGNoiseInjectionLatent outputs the injected model which both KSamplers receive

the double KSampler setup works like a hires fix but cleaner:

KSampler 1 — 30 steps, cfg 3, euler/simple, denoise 1.0 (full generation)

latent goes through LatentUpscaleBy at 1.5x (nearest-exact)

KSampler 2 — 15 steps, cfg 3, euler/simple, denoise 0.5 (refine pass)

both samplers share the same seed via a PrimitiveNode set to increment, which means every run the seed steps up by 1 and both samplers stay in sync without you having to manually manage it. clean, repeatable.

drawbacks

LGNoiseInjection is pretty sensitive — too high a strength and it starts fighting the prompt, you'll see compositional drift

the double sampler adds significant inference time especially at this res, not ideal if you're batch running a lot of gens

nearest-exact upscale before the second pass can occasionally introduce tiling artefacts depending on the model — worth testing bicubic if you see it

overall the combo of kjnodes GenerateNoise + LG injection is a solid alternative to the standard empty latent → sampler pipeline, especially for models like Z-Image where noise structure matters more than usual. the double pass at 1.5x is basically a free quality bump if you have the time budget for it.

This workflow takes a static portrait image and an audio file and outputs a fully animated talking head video, with facial movement and lip sync driven entirely by the audio. It's built on Wan 2.1's Image-to-Video 14B model combined with the InfiniteTalk MultiTalk extension.

How it works:

Your input image gets resized via ImageResizeKJv2 to your target res (default 854x480)

The audio passes through MelBandRoFormer first — a vocal separator that strips out music/instruments and isolates the voice before it hits the animation pipeline

MultiTalkWav2VecEmbeds converts the isolated vocal audio into motion embeddings via Wav2Vec2, which are what actually drive the facial animation

WanVideoImageToVideoMultiTalk + WanVideoSampler handle the I2V generation, using InfiniteTalk mode to loop through all required frames in windows — meaning it can generate video as long as your audio is, not just a fixed clip

Max frames is manually set (25fps, so audio_seconds × 25 = max_frames)

Output is H264 MP4 with the original audio muxed in via VHS_VideoCombine

Optional upscale stage:
A full SeedVR2 (7B) video upscaling pipeline is included at the end — pre-scales the frames with ImageScaleBy at 1.25x, then passes through SeedVR2VideoUpscaler targeting 1920px, outputting a separate GIF.

Models required: Wan 2.1 I2V 14B (fp8 or GGUF), InfiniteTalk MultiTalk model, Wav2Vec2, MelBandRoFormer vocal separator, CLIP Vision H, WanVideo VAE — all download links are included in the workflow's notes panel. A LightX2V LoRA (step distillation) is also baked in for faster sampling.

node of the day: ClownsharKSampler (RES4LYF)

on the surface it looks like a bloated KSampler with a funny name. in practice it's a completely different animal.

the standard KSampler gives you ~10 sampler options and a scheduler. ClownsharKSampler gives you 115+ samplers, 24 noise types, 11 noise scaling modes, and a handful of parameters that simply don't exist in core ComfyUI. here's the ones worth knowing:

eta

this is the big one. eta controls how much noise gets injected after each step during SDE sampling — KSampler has no equivalent, it handles noise injection internally and you never touch it. setting eta to 0 makes it behave like a pure ODE sampler (deterministic, no mid-step noise). anything above 0 starts adding stochastic noise per step. in this workflow i'm running it at 0.15 — low enough to keep the output clean but still getting the variance benefits of SDE. crank it too high and you'll start seeing oversaturation and loss of detail fast.

bongmath

yes that's a real parameter. it enables advanced noise calculations during the sampling pass that alter how the noise schedule is computed internally. the short version: it tends to improve coherence and detail retention, especially at lower step counts. community consensus seems to be 20-30 steps + eta 0.5 + bongmath true = solid starting point for most models, though in this workflow i'm running it at 8 steps with denoise 0.30 as a refinement pass, so lower settings make more sense here.

sampler_name differences

the regular KSampler has euler — ClownsharKSampler has linear/euler, res_2m, res_3s, deis_2m, and dozens more. the RES family specifically is worth exploring — it's a refined version of the dpmpp family built for rectified flow models, and typically reaches the same quality as dpmpp in fewer steps. samplers ending in s use substeps (more model calls, more accurate), m are multistep (Euler speed, recycles previous steps as estimates).

steps vs steps_to_run

another thing KSampler doesn't have. steps sets the total schedule, steps_to_run is how many you actually execute. useful if you want to define a full 30-step sigma curve but only run the first 8 — lets you control exactly which part of the denoising trajectory you're sampling from without needing external sigma manipulation nodes.

drawbacks

the sampler list is overwhelming until you actually read what the suffixes mean (s = substeps, m = multistep)

eta is deceptively sensitive — the difference between 0.1 and 0.5 is massive depending on your scheduler

bongmath + high eta + a slow _s sampler will make your gen times hurt, especially on smaller GPUs

it's from RES4LYF which is a large node pack — adds a decent amount of overhead to your install if you're only using this one node

overall one of the more genuinely useful custom samplers i've used. the eta + bongmath combo alone gives you control over the stochastic behaviour that core ComfyUI just doesn't expose. worth installing the full RES4LYF pack just to have access to it.

A lot of people have asked me what models, i used to create most of my images, i have already posted my preferred Z-Image model in the community Redcraft. But there is a crucial step in my base image generation that i didnt mention, and thats Z image Turbo

Z image Turbo is by far the best for its Hyper realism in the zimage Family of models.

so why even bother with Z image Base? 

Because Z image turbo struggle with variety and differentiation between seeds, Zimage base helps create the 'idea'/concept of the image, the passing it over to z image turbo allows for that hyper realism to be added.

There are a few ways you can apply this to comfy ui;

you can do whats called split-step diffusion where you generate the first several steps of the image with Ziamge base, then the last steps with Zimage turbo. 

 I prefer a different approach because it gives me more control over each pass independently by running two full passes (Base → Turbo) — the output of the first full generation becomes the input of the second, with a reduced denoise strength. Here's why i like to do it:

- More coherent base structure — the first full pass locks in a complete latent, not a half-baked one
    
- Turbo as a refiner/stylizer — running Turbo at low denoise (~0.4–0.55) over a Base output is similar to how the official documentation recommends using the model 
    
- Independent CFG/sampler settings — each pass can be fully tuned without the sigma-splitting constraints of the advanced sampler (my main reason)

*What I Learnt With Video Generation:*

1. The Foundation: The key to high-quality videos is the base images. It sounds like common sense now that I think about it, but it was a genuine part of video gen that I completely missed during my first couple of NSFW scripts.

2. Quality over Speed: Generating high quality at a slow speed is vastly superior to generating with lightning LoRAs. *Ideal Settings for 8 Seconds of High-Quality Video:*

* Steps: 25
* Split Step: 10
* Frames: 121
* Interpolation: x 4
* Overlap Frame: 1 (very important to decrease that visible transition from the first vid to the second)
* Frame Rate: 60

These settings give amazing results. One of the biggest tips would be focusing on close-up NSFW videos. Close-ups always look more real because the AI model is allowed to focus more on the anatomy and face. When extending videos with the face as a close-up, the model’s face will degrade less over time compared to extending videos where the face is far away.

3. The IPAdapter Rule: Always use an IPAdapter for your model’s face when generating extensions to your videos. You will need to generate an extremely high-quality portrait of your model’s face. I personally use Nano Banana to get this, just because the grain that Z-image often produces will conflict with the IPAdapter. If the IPAdapter sees grain on the model’s face, it will attach that grain to the model's identity. 💯

🔥 PiD is a new decoder that immediately turns latent into a 2K / 4K image

Nvidia researchers have introduced the Pixel Diffusion Decoder, where the decoder does not work like a regular VAE, but like a pixel-space diffusion model.

Usually the scheme is as follows:
latent — VAE decoder — image — upscaler

PiD does it differently:
latent — diffusion decoder — immediately high-res image

What it can do:
— 4× upscale up to 2048px
— 4K decoding (up to 3840px)
— works in just 4 diffusion steps (distilled checkpoints)
— there are versions for Flux1/Z-Image, Flux2, SD3, DINOv2, SigLIP

The PiD tries to finish the final high-res image immediately during the decoding process, preserving more details.
